Methodology

How the tracker identifies and classifies legislation

This page explains the tracker’s scope, official sources, review process, classification rules, evidence standards, and limitations.

Methodology reviewed August 31, 2026

Scope of review

What the public tracker includes

The tracker reviews current and recent measures from the U.S. Congress, all 50 states, and the District of Columbia, together with enacted laws that remain relevant to its scope. A measure is considered for inclusion when its operative text creates, authorizes, or conditions a legal consequence for an individual through at least one of the following classifications:

  • Chinese nationality, citizenship, residence, ancestry, ethnicity, or national origin stated in the text;
  • a broader category—such as “foreign adversary,” “country of concern,” or “national of a covered country”—whose incorporated definition includes China; or
  • a nationality-neutral individual classification, such as a specified immigration status, that the measure expressly applies to Chinese nationals.

Selection is based on the legal reach of the text, not a sponsor’s presumed motive, the measure’s political support, or its predicted likelihood of passage. Full-text keyword matches are the primary discovery method, but candidates may also be identified through manual review or reports from readers. The route by which a candidate is found does not determine whether it qualifies.

Included after review

Measures regulating an individual’s property, education, research access, employment, contracting, immigration, finance, benefits, civil liberties, or another legal interest through a covered classification.

Excluded from public results

Commendations and protective civil-rights measures; measures directed only at governments, companies, procurement, trade, sanctions, or export controls; and keyword matches without a qualifying individual legal effect.

Sources and collection

Official text is the primary evidence

Federal corpus

Bill and resolution text comes from the U.S. Government Publishing Office’s GovInfo Congressional Bills collection. “BILLS” is the collection’s data code.

Titles, summaries, actions, and status come from the official Congress.gov Bill Status XML collection. The current review covers both sessions of the 119th Congress, beginning January 3, 2025.

State corpus

State scrapers query official legislature websites for all 50 states and the District of Columbia. Depending on the source, they search an official full-text index or enumerate official HTML and PDF bill documents and search extracted text locally.

At the latest search-depth verification, 50 jurisdictions had live-verified full-text discovery. Indiana’s full-text path is implemented but awaits live re-verification because the official site returned its application shell instead of bill data during the August 18, 2026 check.

Source and failure policy: Public bill links must lead to official government sources. Secondary aggregators and news reports are not accepted as bill sources. A source outage, malformed response, or failed document extraction is reported as an error; it is never treated as a search with zero results.

Review process

Candidates come from full-text searches and human reports

  1. Identify candidates

    The primary method searches full bill text using keyword groups covering identity, foreign-adversary definitions, property, education, research, employment, immigration, finance, infrastructure, benefits, and civil liberties. Reviewers may also select measures manually, and readers may report measures for consideration. A search match or human report is a lead, not evidence that a measure qualifies.

  2. Confirm identity and text version

    The reviewer verifies the jurisdiction, session, bill number, official bill page, and the text version that supports the record. Companion measures remain distinct records.

  3. Read the operative provisions

    Review focuses on definitions, incorporated lists, citizenship and lawful permanent resident exemptions, covered conduct, geographic scope, penalties, divestment requirements, and enforcement authority.

  4. Verify status and classify

    Official actions are checked before a human reviewer assigns scope, tier, category, affected group, and public status. Ambiguous federal candidates may remain visibly labeled “scope review pending.”

Classification

Tiers describe the population the text can reach

Tiers record the outer reach of the measure’s definitions. They are neither rankings of severity nor estimates of political importance, and Tier 1 does not mean that every Chinese American is covered.

Tier 1

Lawful permanent residents (LPRs) and/or citizens

The operative language can individually restrict lawful permanent residents or U.S. citizens, or it lacks a reliable exemption removing those groups.

Tier 2

Visa applicants, visa holders, and other non-LPRs

The measure directly restricts visa applicants, visa holders, nonresident aliens, or other foreign nationals while exempting or not reaching citizens and lawful permanent residents.

Categories describe the primary restriction.

Research access and security is separate from employment because some measures regulate laboratory, data, visitor, or institutional access without creating an employment rule. Measures principally regulating hiring or contracts remain under employment and contracting. Mixed measures are assigned to the category that best describes their central operative provision, with other effects explained in the record.

Scope review is separate from legislative status.

The federal page includes some high-signal measures requiring a final scope decision, especially where coverage turns on PRC government, Chinese Communist Party, employment, agency, or other conduct-based affiliation rather than nationality alone.

Evidence standards

Claims are limited to what the cited evidence supports

Legal effect

Descriptions of who is covered and what is restricted must be traceable to operative text, definitions, incorporated authorities, and exemptions in an identified bill version.

Practical or disparate effect

A claim about real-world impact requires a dated, cited source and is labeled as context. It is not presented as the measure’s direct legal effect.

Motive and intent

The tracker does not infer a sponsor’s motive from a keyword, country reference, or predicted impact. Statements about intent require direct evidence and attribution.

Uncertainty and correction

Unresolved classifications are labeled rather than presented as settled. Automated checks test data shape, totals, page structure, scraper behavior, and official-link policy; reported corrections are reviewed against the source record.

Status and counting rules

What the labels mean

Pending describes a measure’s legislative status. Scope review pending describes the tracker’s editorial classification and does not imply legislative activity.

Enacted

Signed, otherwise became law, or published as an enacted measure.

Pending

Not enacted and not formally failed. This does not imply momentum; a bill referred to committee with no further action remains pending while its Congress or legislative session is open.

Failed

Vetoed, defeated, indefinitely postponed, declared inexpedient, or otherwise did not advance before the relevant deadline.

  • House and Senate companion bills are counted as separate measures. A proposal family groups substantively related companion measures for context without merging their legislative records.
  • State measures and federal measures are counted separately; federal candidates never change the state map.
  • The historical items discussed on the Why We Track This page are excluded from dashboard totals. Repealed laws are not counted as current tracked measures.

Updates and limitations

How current the tracker is

State monitoring

The state discovery and status tools are run periodically, with an operational goal of every few days. They report new candidates and status changes for review; they do not automatically publish edits to the website. The current public state dataset was last fully reviewed August 18, 2026.

A state card’s “Record updated” date is tracker metadata indicating when that record was last revised or verified; it is not necessarily the legislature’s latest action date.

Federal monitoring

The federal classifications reflect a point-in-time full-text sweep reviewed August 21, 2026. Official legislative actions and committee assignments were refreshed August 26, 2026. Recurring federal collection is planned but is not yet automated.

Limitations: Legislative websites and schemas change; some sources cap search results; text may be published after introduction; optical or HTML extraction may fail; incorporated federal lists can change; and reasonable readers can disagree about legal effect. Search terms can produce false positives and can miss relevant language. The tracker is an informational research project, not legal advice.
